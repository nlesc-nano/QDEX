Part 8: Multi-Carrier Auger Recombination in Quantum Dots
=========================================================

In colloidal semiconductor quantum dots and nanocrystals, multi-carrier excitations—such as neutral biexcitons (:math:`XX`), charged trions (:math:`X^-`, :math:`X^+`), and hot carriers generated by high-power laser fluences—experience rapid non-radiative decay via **Auger Recombination**.

Auger recombination is the primary non-radiative pathway that quenches photoluminescence quantum yield (PLQY) in light-emitting diodes (LEDs), sets the threshold gain for colloidal quantum dot lasers, and governs multi-exciton harvesting in third-generation photovoltaic devices.

``QDEX`` implements a rigorous, high-performance Auger recombination engine based on **Fermi's Golden Rule**, atom-centered transition charges, and the microscopic **Resta-screened dielectric kernel**. This eliminates the catastrophic :math:`O(N_{\mathrm{ao}}^5)` scaling of conventional four-center electron repulsion integrals, enabling fast, quantitative calculations for nanocrystals containing thousands of atoms.

---

1. Physical Foundations of Auger Scattering in Quantum Dots
------------------------------------------------------------

Auger recombination is a three-carrier Coulomb scattering process wherein an electron and a hole recombine non-radiatively, transferring the released bandgap energy (:math:`\Delta E_{eh} \approx E_g`) to a third "spectator" carrier that is kicked high into the continuum:

.. list-table::
   :widths: 22 38 40
   :header-rows: 1

   * - Recombination Channel
     - Carrier Process
     - Final State & Signature
   * - **Negative Trion (:math:`eeh`)**
     - :math:`e_2 + h \to 0`, ejecting spectator electron :math:`e_1 \to e'`
     - Hot electron in conduction band continuum (:math:`\sim \varepsilon_{\mathrm{LUMO}} + E_g`)
   * - **Positive Trion (:math:`hhe`)**
     - :math:`h_2 + e \to 0`, ejecting spectator hole :math:`h_1 \to h'`
     - Hot hole deep in valence band continuum (:math:`\sim \varepsilon_{\mathrm{HOMO}} - E_g`)
   * - **Neutral Biexciton (:math:`XX`)**
     - Simultaneous presence of 2 electrons and 2 holes
     - Net decay: :math:`\Gamma_{XX} = 2 \Gamma_{eeh} + 2 \Gamma_{hhe}`, :math:`\tau_{XX} = 1 / \Gamma_{XX}`

Breakdown of Momentum Conservation & :math:`1/V^2` Volume Scaling
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

In bulk periodic crystals, Auger recombination is constrained by strict **crystal momentum conservation**:

.. math::

   \mathbf{k}_1 + \mathbf{k}_2 = \mathbf{k}_3 + \mathbf{k}_4

Because the energy transferred is large (:math:`\approx E_g`), the final state must have a large wavevector :math:`|\mathbf{k}_f| \approx \sqrt{2 m^* E_g} / \hbar`, which imposes an exponential thermal activation threshold:

.. math::

   \Gamma_{\mathrm{Auger}}^{\mathrm{bulk}} \propto \exp\left( -\frac{\Delta E_{\mathrm{threshold}}}{k_B T} \right)

In colloidal quantum dots, the spatial confinement of the envelope wavefunctions :math:`\psi(\mathbf{r})` within an effective volume :math:`V = \frac{4}{3}\pi R^3` breaks translational symmetry. In reciprocal space, the wavefunctions possess a wide spread of Fourier components:

.. math::

   \Delta k \sim \frac{\pi}{R}

This **removes the momentum conservation requirement**, allowing the transition to proceed with zero activation energy. 

Furthermore, because the two-body Coulomb matrix element scales inversely with the confinement volume:

.. math::

   M_{if} \propto \frac{1}{V} \implies |M_{if}|^2 \propto \frac{1}{V^2} \propto \frac{1}{R^6}

Auger recombination rates in quantum dots increase dramatically with decreasing size:

.. math::

   \Gamma_{\mathrm{Auger}} \propto \frac{1}{R^6} \quad \left( \text{or } \tau_{XX} \propto V \propto R^3 \text{ when integrated over continuum DOS} \right)

Consequently, biexciton lifetimes in colloidal QDs fall into the sub-nanosecond regime (:math:`\tau_{XX} \sim 10 - 100\text{ ps}`), outcompeting spontaneous radiative recombination (:math:`\tau_{\mathrm{rad}} \sim 10 - 50\text{ ns}`) by three orders of magnitude.

2. Many-Body Formulation & Matrix Elements
------------------------------------------

Within first-order time-dependent perturbation theory (**Fermi's Golden Rule**), the transition rate from an initial multi-carrier state :math:`|i\rangle` to a continuum manifold of final states :math:`|f\rangle` is:

.. math::

   \Gamma_{\mathrm{Auger}} = \frac{2\pi}{\hbar} \sum_f \left| M_{if} \right|^2 \delta(E_i - E_f)

The Two-Body Screened Coulomb Operator
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The interaction driving the transition is the screened two-body Coulomb operator:

.. math::

   \hat{W} = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{pqrs} \langle pq | \hat{W} | rs \rangle \, c_p^\dagger c_q^\dagger c_s c_r

For the negative trion / biexciton :math:`eeh` channel:
* Initial 3-carrier state: :math:`|i\rangle = c_{e_1}^\dagger c_{e_2}^\dagger c_h |0\rangle`
* Final 1-carrier state: :math:`|f\rangle = c_{e'}^\dagger |0\rangle`

Evaluating the matrix element :math:`M_{if} = \langle f | \hat{W} | i \rangle` yields an anti-symmetrized pair of direct and exchange two-electron integrals:

.. math::

   M_{if} = V_{e' e_1, h e_2} - V_{e' e_2, h e_1}

where each two-electron orbital integral is defined as:

.. math::

   V_{e' e_1, h e_2} = \iint \phi_{e'}^*(\mathbf{r}_1) \, \phi_{e_1}(\mathbf{r}_1) \; W(\mathbf{r}_1, \mathbf{r}_2) \; \phi_h^*(\mathbf{r}_2) \, \phi_{e_2}(\mathbf{r}_2) \; d\mathbf{r}_1 \, d\mathbf{r}_2

Notice the physical factorization of the two coordinate spaces:
1. :math:`\rho_{\mathrm{recomb}}(\mathbf{r}_2) = \phi_h^*(\mathbf{r}_2) \phi_{e_2}(\mathbf{r}_2)` is the **recombination transition density** of the annihilating electron-hole pair.
2. :math:`\rho_{\mathrm{eject}}(\mathbf{r}_1) = \phi_{e'}^*(\mathbf{r}_1) \phi_{e_1}(\mathbf{r}_1)` is the **ejection transition density** describing excitation of the spectator carrier into the continuum.

3. The Atom-Centered Resta Screening Contraction
------------------------------------------------

In standard quantum chemistry, evaluating :math:`V_{e' e_1, h e_2}` requires transforming four-center two-electron atomic orbital integrals :math:`(\mu \nu | \hat{W} | \lambda \sigma)`:

.. math::

   V_{e' e_1, h e_2} = \sum_{\mu \nu \lambda \sigma} C_{\mu e'}^* C_{\nu e_1} C_{\lambda h}^* C_{\sigma e_2} (\mu \nu | \hat{W} | \lambda \sigma)

For a nanocrystal with :math:`N_{\mathrm{ao}} = 10,000` basis functions, storing and transforming :math:`(\mu \nu | \lambda \sigma)` requires :math:`O(N_{\mathrm{ao}}^5)` operations and petabytes of memory, rendering *ab initio* Auger calculations completely intractable.

Monopole Transition Charge Projection
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

To solve this scaling bottleneck, ``QDEX`` contracts the transition densities into atom-centered point charges using Mulliken population analysis:

.. math::

   q_A^{e_2 h} = \sum_{\lambda \in A} \sum_{\sigma=1}^{N_{\mathrm{ao}}} C_{\lambda e_2} S_{\lambda \sigma} C_{\sigma h}

.. math::

   q_B^{e' e_1} = \sum_{\mu \in B} \sum_{\nu=1}^{N_{\mathrm{ao}}} C_{\mu e'} S_{\mu \nu} C_{\nu e_1}

The continuous six-dimensional integral collapses into a fast, pairwise contraction over atomic sites :math:`A` and :math:`B`:

.. math::

   V_{\mathrm{dir}} = \sum_{A=1}^{N_{\mathrm{atoms}}} \sum_{B=1}^{N_{\mathrm{atoms}}} q_A^{e_2 h} \; W_{AB}^{\mathrm{Resta}} \; q_B^{e' e_1} = (\mathbf{q}^{e_2 h})^T \, \mathbf{W}^{\mathrm{Resta}} \, \mathbf{q}^{e' e_1}

.. math::

   V_{\mathrm{exch}} = \sum_{A=1}^{N_{\mathrm{atoms}}} \sum_{B=1}^{N_{\mathrm{atoms}}} q_A^{e_1 h} \; W_{AB}^{\mathrm{Resta}} \; q_B^{e' e_2} = (\mathbf{q}^{e_1 h})^T \, \mathbf{W}^{\mathrm{Resta}} \, \mathbf{q}^{e' e_2}

The Microscopic Resta Screening Kernel
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The interaction matrix :math:`W_{AB}^{\mathrm{Resta}}` is evaluated using the **Resta model of electronic screening**:

.. math::

   W_{AB}^{\mathrm{Resta}} = \frac{1}{\epsilon_\infty \sqrt{R_{AB}^2 + a_{AB}^2}} + \frac{1 - \epsilon_\infty^{-1}}{\sqrt{R_{AB}^2 + a_{AB}^2}} \exp\left( -\frac{\sqrt{R_{AB}^2 + a_{AB}^2}}{\lambda_s} \right)

where:
* :math:`\epsilon_\infty` is the bulk optical dielectric constant.
* :math:`a_{AB} = 2 / (\eta_A + \eta_B)` is the Ohno-Klopman damping parameter derived from atomic chemical hardness values.
* :math:`\lambda_s` is the valence Thomas-Fermi screening length:

.. math::

   \lambda_s = \left( \frac{\pi}{4 (3\pi^2 n_v)^{1/3}} \right)^{1/2}

Physical Superiority for Auger Scattering
"""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""
At short intra-atomic distances (:math:`R_{AB} \to 0`), the Resta kernel recovers the **unscreened vacuum interaction** (:math:`\epsilon \to 1`), accurately representing the intense, short-range electron-electron collisions that drive Auger ejection. At long distances (:math:`R_{AB} \gg \lambda_s`), it seamlessly converges to macroscopic dielectric polarization (:math:`W \to 1 / (\epsilon_\infty R)`).

4. Energy Conservation & Continuum Discretization
-------------------------------------------------

In Fermi's Golden Rule, the Dirac delta function :math:`\delta(E_i - E_f)` enforces strict energy conservation:

.. math::

   E_f = E_i \implies \varepsilon_{e'}^{\mathrm{QP}} - \varepsilon_{e_1}^{\mathrm{QP}} \approx \varepsilon_{e_2}^{\mathrm{QP}} - \varepsilon_h^{\mathrm{QP}} = \Delta E_{eh} \approx E_g

The ejected carrier must land in a virtual state located **approximately one bandgap :math:`E_g` above the conduction band edge** (or one :math:`E_g` below the valence band edge).

In standard DFT calculations utilizing localized atom-centered Gaussian basis sets (such as DZVP-MOLOPT), the virtual spectrum consists of discrete eigenvalues rather than an infinite continuum. ``QDEX`` provides two rigorous methods to evaluate the energy conservation factor:

1. Gaussian Line Shape Model
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

When thermal vibrations and homogeneous broadening at finite temperature are modeled phenomenologically:

.. math::

   \delta(E_i - E_f) \approx \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma} \exp\left( -\frac{(E_i - E_f)^2}{2\sigma^2} \right)

where :math:`\sigma` (configured via ``--auger-sigma``, default :math:`0.05\text{ eV}`) represents thermal and vibronic dispersion.

2. Multi-Phonon Marcus / Jortner Line Shape (FCWD)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

In realistic quantum dots, Auger recombination is **phonon-assisted**: when the electronic energy mismatch :math:`\Delta E = E_i - E_f` is non-zero, lattice vibrations absorb or supply the energy discrepancy.

Using the **Franck-Condon Weighted Density (FCWD)**:

.. math::

   \operatorname{FCWD}(E_i - E_f) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi \lambda k_B T}} \exp\left( -\frac{(E_i - E_f - \lambda)^2}{4\lambda k_B T} \right)

where the nuclear reorganization energy :math:`\lambda` and optical phonon energy :math:`\hbar \omega_{\mathrm{LO}}` are extracted directly from the **Phonon Spectral Density** :math:`J(\omega)` of the NAMD trajectory (see Part 6).

5. Relativistic Spin-Orbit Coupling (SOC) in Auger Recombination
----------------------------------------------------------------

When heavy elements (Pb, Bi, I) are present, Spin-Orbit Coupling splits degenerate valence and conduction manifolds into two-component relativistic spinors:

.. math::

   \psi_k(\mathbf{r}) = \begin{pmatrix} \psi_k^\alpha(\mathbf{r}) \\ \psi_k^\beta(\mathbf{r}) \end{pmatrix}

``QDEX`` generalizes transition charge calculation to the spinor basis:

.. math::

   q_A^{IJ} = \sum_{\mu \in A} \sum_{\nu=1}^{N_{\mathrm{ao}}} \left[ (U_{\mu I}^\alpha)^* S_{\mu \nu} U_{\nu J}^\alpha + (U_{\mu I}^\beta)^* S_{\mu \nu} U_{\nu J}^\beta \right]

Because the spinor coefficients mix spin-up and spin-down components, the anti-symmetrized amplitude :math:`M_{if} = V_{\mathrm{dir}} - V_{\mathrm{exch}}` **automatically captures all spin-flip Auger pathways**, eliminating empirical spin selection rules.

Trajectory-Averaged Dynamic Auger Rates in NAMD
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

During an *ab initio* molecular dynamics (AIMD) trajectory, orbital energies and wavefunctions fluctuate due to thermal lattice motion. ``QDEX`` evaluates the instantaneous Auger rate :math:`\Gamma_{XX}(t_k)` at each trajectory time step and computes the trajectory average:

.. math::

   \langle \Gamma_{XX} \rangle = \frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{frames}}} \sum_{k=1}^{N_{\mathrm{frames}}} \Gamma_{XX}(t_k)

This trajectory averaging provides key physical advantages:

* **Removes the need for empirical broadening**: Thermal fluctuations naturally sample energy-conserving crossings between discrete virtual levels and the target energy :math:`\varepsilon_{\mathrm{LUMO}} + E_g`.
* **Couples into the Multi-Exciton Master Equation**: Under high-fluence conditions, the population of neutral biexcitons decays via Auger recombination into hot single excitons:

.. math::

   \frac{d P_{XX}}{dt} = - \left( \Gamma_{XX} + 2 k_{\mathrm{rad}} \right) P_{XX}

.. math::

   \frac{d P_{X^*}}{dt} = \Gamma_{XX} P_{XX} - k_{\mathrm{cool}} P_{X^*}

The hot carrier then cools down to the band edge on the carrier cooling timescale (:math:`\tau_{\mathrm{cool}} \sim 100 - 500\text{ fs}`), followed by single-exciton radiative decay (:math:`\tau_{\mathrm{rad}} \sim 10\text{ ns}`).

7. Configuration Reference (YAML & CLI)
---------------------------------------

YAML Configuration Options
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

.. code-block:: yaml

   auger:
     run: true               # Enable Auger recombination calculation
     sigma: 0.05             # Energy conservation broadening width in eV
     channel: "all"          # "all" (both eeh and hhe), "eeh", or "hhe"
     n_initial_states: 1     # Number of frontier band-edge carriers to consider
     lineshape: "gaussian"   # "gaussian" or "fcwd"

Command-Line Arguments
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* ``--auger``: Enable Auger recombination rate calculation.
* ``--auger-sigma <float>``: Energy conservation broadening width in eV (default: ``0.05``).
* ``--auger-channel {all,eeh,hhe}``: Recombination channel to compute (default: ``all``).
* ``--auger-states <int>``: Number of band-edge frontier states to consider as initial carriers (default: ``1``).
* ``--auger-lineshape {gaussian,fcwd}``: Energy conservation line shape (default: ``gaussian``).
